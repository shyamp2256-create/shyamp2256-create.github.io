01 / ABOUT ME

Understanding the idea.
Then making it work.

My interests sit at the intersection of hardware, mathematics, and the information that connects them.

Georgia Institute of Technology

Circuit Technology

My curiosity about how things work started with a bucket of padlocks and keys. As a child, I would take locks apart to understand their mechanisms. The pieces did not always end up back together, but the experience made me want to look beneath the surface and understand how individual parts create a working system.

That curiosity followed me into architecture and engineering design at Union Grove High School. What began as an elective became an opportunity to explore why buildings are organized in certain ways and how design choices affect the people using a space. Through an American Institute of Architects project, a client house design, and a dream house project, I practiced translating ideas into floor plans, dimensions, layouts, and exterior views.

Mathematics gave me another way to approach the same questions. I enjoyed finding the structure behind a complicated problem and connecting an equation to something concrete. Electrical engineering brings these interests together: the creativity of design, the precision of mathematics, and the challenge of making a system function.

I am now pursuing a Bachelor of Science in Electrical Engineering at Georgia Tech, with graduation expected in May 2028. My interests include circuit technology, signal processing, embedded systems, and semiconductor engineering. I am also part of the Agile Locomotion & Manipulation Vertically Integrated Projects team, where I am beginning to build experience with collaborative engineering and research workflows.

Leadership has been another important part of my development. In high school, I served as SkillsUSA president, NJROTC battalion operations officer, and Mu Alpha Theta vice president. These roles gave me opportunities to coordinate activities, support other students, and practice clear communication. Volunteering as a head volunteer at the Ambaji USA Shree Shakti Mandir youth summer camp also connected that leadership to service.

My front-desk work at Baymont by Wyndham gives me a different setting to practice those skills. Handling reservations, check-ins, payments, and guest concerns requires organization and attention to detail. I want to carry those habits into engineering: understand the problem, communicate with the people involved, and follow through carefully.

02 / AREAS OF INTEREST

Three connected directions.

The questions I want to keep exploring as my technical foundation grows.

01 / CIRCUITS

From components to systems

I want to understand how component choices affect the behavior of an entire circuit. I am especially interested in connecting analysis with measurement: predicting what should happen, observing what actually happens, and investigating the difference. Developing that habit will help me approach more complex hardware with clearer reasoning.

02 / SEMICONDUCTORS

The technology underneath

Semiconductors interest me because they connect physical principles with large-scale computation. I want to learn how device behavior influences circuit design and how design tradeoffs shape performance. Building a stronger understanding of both devices and digital systems will help me identify which part of chip development I would like to pursue.

03 / SIGNAL PROCESSING

Finding meaning in signals

I am interested in how mathematical methods help engineers interpret real-world information. Signals connect physical measurements to useful decisions, whether the goal is identifying a pattern, reducing noise, or communicating data. I want to strengthen the mathematical and computational skills needed to reason about those tasks carefully.
